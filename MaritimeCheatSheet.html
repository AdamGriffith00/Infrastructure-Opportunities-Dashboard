Scope of Spend Figures – Addressable Market (UK Ports & Maritime)

  • Port company CAPEX (major private and trust ports such as ABP, Peel, PD Ports, Forth Ports, Port of Tyne, Dover, Belfast) is the core driver of spend on quays, berths, terminals, logistics parks and energy hubs.
  • Public-sector coastal & resilience programmes – led by the Environment Agency and Local Authorities – fund sea defences, flood risk schemes and harbour protection that are often tightly coupled to port estates and access channels.
  • Freeports, levelling-up and city-region programmes provide additional investment for enabling infrastructure, road/rail access, skills and logistics zones around key ports.
  • Offshore wind and wider offshore energy continue to drive quay strengthening, laydown areas, assembly and O&M bases, particularly in the North East, East & Anglia and Scotland.
  • Decarbonisation and energy transition – including shore power, alternative fuels (hydrogen/ammonia, methanol), on-site renewables and grid connections – are emerging as a significant multi-year investment theme.
  • Digital and automation (terminal operating systems, port community systems, traffic management, remote monitoring) add a layer of systems and facilities work alongside civils and M&E upgrades.
  • This sheet aims to provide a whole-system UK ports & maritime view of the addressable market around 2026, combining port-led investment, public coastal programmes and offshore-linked infrastructure. Values are indicative and should be updated as Freeport, offshore wind and decarbonisation decisions move forward.

2026 Addressable Market – One-year View (indicative)

Bringing together port-company CAPEX, public coastal programmes and offshore energy interfaces, the 2026 UK ports & maritime infrastructure market is expected to sit in the region of £2.5–4.0bn per year. The table below shows an indicative breakdown by segment.

Segment Illustrative 2026 infrastructure spend Notes (what's in scope)
Quays, berths & jetties (civils/structures) c.£0.9–1.4bn New build and strengthening of quays, berths, dolphins and jetties for cargo, ferry and offshore energy uses; often phased across estates.
Dredging & navigation (capital/maintenance) c.£0.3–0.5bn Capital and maintenance dredging campaigns, navigation channel works and disposal, constrained by environmental windows and consents.
Logistics & inland connectivity (warehousing/rail/road) c.£0.4–0.6bn Port-centric logistics parks, warehousing, intermodal rail terminals and road access improvements linked to freeports and growth corridors.
Coastal defence & resilience interfaces c.£0.3–0.5bn Sea walls, breakwaters and flood risk schemes delivered by EA/LAs where they protect or enhance port estates and harbours.
Terminal equipment & M&E (cranes, linkspans, fenders) c.£0.2–0.3bn STS and yard cranes, linkspans, RoRo ramps, fenders, M&E and associated buildings upgrades.
Digital/automation & port community systems c.£0.15–0.25bn Terminal operating systems, automation, gate & traffic management, asset monitoring and data platforms.
Energy & decarbonisation (shore power/alt fuels) c.£0.15–0.30bn Shore power installations, grid reinforcement, on-site renewables and alternative fuel infrastructure pilots and roll-out.
Offshore wind O&M / assembly interfaces c.£0.1–0.2bn Quay strengthening, laydown and O&M facilities directly tied to offshore wind and other offshore energy projects.
Total indicative UK ports & maritime infrastructure & systems c.£2.5–4.0bn Aligned with the 1-year spend KPI; provides a whole-system view for planning and opportunity scanning around 2026.
